# Slice privacy policy

_Last updated: 26 November 2026_

Slice is an Atlassian Forge app that helps hospital safety teams learn from near-miss reports in Jira
and Confluence. This policy explains what data Slice uses, where it is kept, and who can see it.

**In short:** Slice runs entirely on Atlassian's Forge platform. It does not send data to any server
outside Atlassian, does not use third-party services, analytics or tracking, and removes patient
identifiers before it stores anything.

## 1. Who we are
Slice is developed by an independent developer for the Atlassian Codegeist 2026 hackathon.
Contact: **mdy8750@gmail.com**

For the data Slice handles inside your Atlassian site, **your organisation is the data controller**,
and Atlassian hosts and processes it under your agreement with Atlassian. The developer has no access
to your site's data.

## 2. What Slice reads
Slice acts only on your Atlassian site, with the permissions shown when it is installed:

- **Jira work items** in the projects an administrator configures as safety projects (default: `SAFE`):
  summary, description, labels, project, status and dates.
- **Confluence pages** labelled `protocol`, or pages in the *Clinical Protocols* space, to check
  protocol review dates.
- **Your Jira permissions**, to show you only reports you can already open, and to check whether
  you are a Jira administrator before you change settings.

## 3. What Slice stores
Slice stores a small amount of data in **Forge storage**, which Atlassian hosts inside your site's
Atlassian cloud environment:

| Stored item | Contents |
|---|---|
| Report records | The issue key, a **redacted** copy of the summary and description, and derived facets (category, location, device, drug, shift, outcome, contributing factors, defences, keywords, event date) |
| Settings | Safety project keys, signal threshold, look-back window, comment setting |
| Alert markers | Which reports have already received a signal comment |
| Feedback | Report pairs a user marked "not related" |
| Action references | Keys and text of action items Slice created, and the report they came from |

**Redaction.** Before storing a report, Slice removes patient names written with titles or after the
word "patient", medical record and hospital numbers, dates of birth, phone numbers, email addresses,
and bed and room numbers. Redaction is pattern-based and cannot catch every possible identifier, so
staff should still follow local policy and keep patient-identifiable information out of near-miss reports.

Slice does **not** store Atlassian account IDs, names or email addresses.

## 4. What Slice writes to your site
Only when a user or administrator asks it to, or as configured:
- A comment and the label `slice-signal` on a report that joins a recurring pattern (can be turned off in settings).
- Jira action items (label `slice-action`) linked to a report, when a user clicks **Create action**.
- Confluence pages: protocol demo pages (**Load demo data**) and safety signal briefs (**Publish brief**).

These are created with the permissions of the user who clicked, or of the app for automatic comments.

## 5. Sharing and transfers
- Slice makes **no external network calls**. Data never leaves Atlassian's platform.
- No data is sold, shared with third parties, or used for advertising or model training.
- On sites with Atlassian Rovo, the Slice agent's answers are generated by Rovo under Atlassian's
  terms. Slice only provides data from its own actions, with the same permission checks as above.

## 6. Retention and deletion
- Report records stay in Forge storage while Slice is installed. They are removed when the matching
  Jira work item is deleted, or when an administrator re-indexes.
- When Slice is uninstalled, its Forge storage is deleted by Atlassian under its Forge data retention
  rules.
- Comments, action items and Confluence pages that Slice created are ordinary site content. They stay
  until your users delete them.

## 7. Security
Slice runs on Atlassian's infrastructure and inherits its security, encryption and data-residency
controls. Access is limited to the scopes listed at install time, and every user-facing view checks
the user's own Jira permissions.

## 8. Not a medical device
Slice supports patient-safety learning and quality improvement. It does not provide clinical advice
and is not a medical device. All demo data is synthetic.
